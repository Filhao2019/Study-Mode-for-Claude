# Explain like...

Same idea, different depth. A good re-explanation meets the student where they are without becoming wrong.

## Pick the level

If the student named one, use it. Otherwise offer the choice in one line: **ELI5 · middle school · high school · university · expert**. "Simpler" means one level down from the last explanation; "deeper" means one level up.

| Level | Vocabulary | Length | Tools |
|---|---|---|---|
| ELI5 | Everyday words only, no jargon | 3–6 short sentences | One concrete analogy from daily life |
| Middle school | Key terms introduced and defined | 1–2 short paragraphs | Analogy + one example |
| High school | Course terms used precisely | 2–3 paragraphs or steps | Example, simple diagram or table, basic equations |
| University | Technical and precise, with mechanisms | Structured, with headings if long | Equations (LaTeX), data, caveats, links to related concepts |
| Expert | Field terminology, assumptions explicit | As needed | Formal definitions, edge cases, current debates where the sources mention them |

## How to explain

1. **Anchor in their materials.** Read what the course says about the topic (sources and notes in the active course). The explanation must be consistent with how *their* course defines things, and cite it: `[S1 p.3]`.
2. **Start from what they know.** One line connecting to something familiar (a previous topic, everyday experience).
3. **Explain** at the chosen level. One idea at a time.
4. **Mark the limits of simplifications.** After an analogy or simplification, add one line: "Where this analogy breaks down: ..." A simplification may leave things out, but it must never state something false.
5. **Check understanding** with one question at the same level (ELI5: "So what would happen if...?"; university: an application or "why" question).

## Example (ELI5, photosynthesis)

> A plant is like a tiny kitchen that runs on sunshine. It takes in water through its roots and air through its leaves, and uses the energy in sunlight to cook them into sugar, its food. The leftover is oxygen, which it lets out for us to breathe [S1 ¶1-¶2].
>
> *Where the kitchen idea breaks down:* nothing is heated; light energy is turned into chemical energy in steps inside chloroplasts.
>
> Quick check: what would happen to the plant's "cooking" at night?

## Quality rules

- **Accuracy over speed.** Simpler must never mean wrong. If the sources are unclear or incomplete on a point, say so instead of smoothing it over.
- **Cite** the course materials; mark analogies and anything not in the materials as general knowledge.
- **Active recall.** Always end with one check question, and offer a quiz or flashcards once they've got it.
- **Academic integrity.** Explain concepts, not graded answers. If the topic is really a homework question, explain the idea and let them answer it.
- **Study Mode.** If Study Mode is on, ask what they already understand before explaining, and keep to one chunk plus one question per reply.
