# IB Biology (SL and HL)

> Starter outline, original summary. Official source: **the IB Biology guide (first assessment 2025)** from your school's IB coordinator or the IB's programme resource centre. Last checked: October 2026.

## Format

| Component | SL | HL | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1: 1A multiple choice + 1B data-based questions | 1 h 30 min | 2 h | 36% |
| Paper 2: data-based and short-answer questions + extended response (SL answers 1 of 2, HL answers 2 of 3) | 1 h 30 min | 2 h 30 min | 44% |
| Internal assessment: scientific investigation (about 10 hours) | | | 20% |

- The course also includes a collaborative sciences project, which isn't graded.
- Final grade 1–7. Grade boundaries change each session.

## Topic outline

The current guide organizes content as four **themes** studied across four **levels of organization** (molecules, cells, organisms, ecosystems). HL adds depth within the same structure.

| Theme (`id`) | Big idea | Examples of content |
|---|---|---|
| A. Unity and diversity (`unity-diversity`) | Living things share common features but vary widely | water, nucleic acids, origins of cells, cell structure, viruses, diversity of organisms, classification, conservation of biodiversity |
| B. Form and function (`form-function`) | Structures are adapted to what they do | carbohydrates, lipids and proteins, membranes, organelles, gas exchange, transport, muscle and motility, adaptation to environment, ecological niches |
| C. Interaction and interdependence (`interaction-interdependence`) | Systems at every level depend on interactions | enzymes and metabolism, cell respiration, photosynthesis, chemical signaling, neural signaling, integration of body systems, defense against disease, populations and communities, energy and matter transfers |
| D. Continuity and change (`continuity-change`) | Life persists and changes over time | DNA replication, protein synthesis, mutation and gene editing, cell and nuclear division, inheritance, homeostasis, natural selection, sustainability and change, climate change |

Use your teacher's sequence for subtopic order: the IB doesn't prescribe a teaching order, and many schools teach through the themes non-linearly.

## Skills the exam rewards

Data analysis (Paper 1B and Paper 2 are data-heavy), applying concepts to unfamiliar examples, command terms used precisely, linking content across themes in extended responses, and the experimental skills of the IA (research question, method, analysis, evaluation).

## Common traps

- Extended responses that list facts without linking them to the question's argument.
- Data questions: describing a trend without quoting values with units, or claiming causation from correlation.
- Ignoring how many marks a question carries: each mark usually needs one distinct, relevant point.
