# AP U.S. History (APUSH)

> Starter outline, original summary. Official source: **AP Central, AP U.S. History CED and exam page** (apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-united-states-history). Last checked: October 2026. **The short-answer and long-essay questions change starting with the May 2027 exam**; course content does not change. The table shows the updated format.

## Format (from May 2027)

| Section | Questions | Time | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| I, Part A: Multiple choice | 55, usually in sets of 3–4 on a primary or secondary source, image, graph or map | 55 min | 40% |
| I, Part B: Short answer (SAQ) | 3, all required, each from a different period: Q1 on a secondary text, Q2 on a primary text, Q3 on a non-text source | 40 min | 20% |
| II: Document-based question (DBQ) | 1, seven documents, a topic between 1754 and 1980 | about 60 min, including a 15-min reading period | 25% |
| II: Long essay (LEQ) | 1 required prompt spanning about half the course's periods; you choose the developments to focus on | about 40 min | 15% |

- Fully digital in the Bluebook app, essays typed.
- Reported on a 1–5 scale. Before May 2027, students chose between SAQ 3 and 4 and chose 1 of 3 LEQ prompts.

## Topic outline and exam weighting

| Period (`id`) | Years | Weight | Themes to track |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (`period-1`) | 1491–1607 | 4–6% | Native societies, contact, the Columbian Exchange, early Spanish colonization |
| 2 (`period-2`) | 1607–1754 | 6–8% | Colonial regions, labor systems and slavery, Atlantic trade, colonial–British relations |
| 3 (`period-3`) | 1754–1800 | 10–17% | Seven Years' War, Revolution, Articles and Constitution, early republic |
| 4 (`period-4`) | 1800–1848 | 10–17% | Democracy and parties, market revolution, reform movements, expansion |
| 5 (`period-5`) | 1844–1877 | 10–17% | Manifest Destiny, sectional crisis, Civil War, Reconstruction |
| 6 (`period-6`) | 1865–1898 | 10–17% | Industrialization, immigration and urbanization, the West, Gilded Age politics |
| 7 (`period-7`) | 1890–1945 | 10–17% | Imperialism, Progressivism, World War I, the 1920s, the Depression and New Deal, World War II |
| 8 (`period-8`) | 1945–1980 | 10–17% | Cold War, civil rights movements, postwar society, Vietnam, the Great Society |
| 9 (`period-9`) | 1980–present | 4–6% | Conservatism, end of the Cold War, globalization, technology, 21st-century debates |

Themes that cut across periods: American and national identity; work, exchange and technology; geography and environment; migration and settlement; politics and power; America in the world; American and regional culture; social structures.

## Skills the exam rewards

Historical thinking skills: developments and processes, sourcing and situation (context, audience, purpose, point of view), claims and evidence in sources, contextualization, making connections (comparison, causation, continuity and change), and argumentation with a defensible thesis.

## Common traps

- SAQs: answering more or less than each part asks; every part needs a specific historical example.
- DBQ: summarizing documents instead of using them as evidence for a claim; skipping sourcing; forgetting outside evidence.
- LEQ: a thesis that restates the prompt instead of taking a position with a line of reasoning.
- Memorizing dates without being able to explain causes and effects.
