# AP Biology

> Starter outline, original summary. Official source: **AP Central, AP Biology Course and Exam Description (CED) and exam page** (apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-biology). Last checked: October 2026 (format for the May 2027 exam).

## Format

| Section | Questions | Time | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| I: Multiple choice | 60 (single questions and sets of 4–5 on a shared stimulus) | 1 h 30 min | 50% |
| II: Free response | 6: 2 long (9 points each, interpreting and evaluating experiments, one with graphing) and 4 short (4 points each: investigation, conceptual analysis, analyzing a model or visual, data analysis) | 1 h 30 min | 50% |

- Hybrid digital: questions on screen in Bluebook, free-response answers handwritten in a booklet.
- A calculator is permitted (check the approved list); a reference sheet with formulas is provided.
- Reported on a 1–5 scale.

## Topic outline and exam weighting

| Unit (`id`) | Weight | Key ideas |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Chemistry of life (`chemistry-of-life`) | 8–11% | water and hydrogen bonding, macromolecules, structure–function of biomolecules, nucleic acids |
| 2. Cell structure and function (`cell-structure-function`) | 10–13% | organelles, surface area to volume, membranes, transport, tonicity, compartmentalization |
| 3. Cellular energetics (`cellular-energetics`) | 12–16% | enzymes, photosynthesis, cellular respiration, fitness and energy |
| 4. Cell communication and cell cycle (`cell-communication-cycle`) | 10–15% | signal transduction, feedback, cell cycle and its regulation |
| 5. Heredity (`heredity`) | 8–11% | meiosis, Mendelian genetics, non-Mendelian inheritance, environmental effects |
| 6. Gene expression and regulation (`gene-expression`) | 12–16% | DNA/RNA structure, replication, transcription, translation, regulation, mutations, biotechnology |
| 7. Natural selection (`natural-selection`) | 13–20% | evolution evidence, Hardy–Weinberg, phylogeny, speciation, extinction |
| 8. Ecology (`ecology`) | 10–15% | responses to environment, energy flow, population and community ecology, biodiversity, disruptions |

## Skills the exam rewards

The CED's science practices: explaining biological concepts and processes, analyzing visual representations and models, questioning and designing experiments, representing and describing data (graphing), statistical tests and data analysis (including chi-square and standard error), and argumentation (claim, evidence, reasoning).

## Common traps

- Describing *what* happens when the question asks to *explain why* (or the reverse); command words matter.
- Experimental design: forgetting to name the independent and dependent variables, a control, or what would be held constant.
- Graphs: missing axis labels and units, wrong graph type, no error bars when data supports them.
- Hardy–Weinberg and chi-square arithmetic under time pressure.
