# AP Chemistry

> Starter outline, original summary. Official source: **AP Central, AP Chemistry CED and exam page** (apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-chemistry). Last checked: October 2026 (format for the May 2027 exam). Unit names below are descriptive; check the current CED for the exact titles.

## Format

| Section | Questions | Time | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| I: Multiple choice | 60 (single questions and sets on shared data or stimulus) | 1 h 30 min | 50% |
| II: Free response | 7: 3 long (10 points each) and 4 short (4 points each) | 1 h 45 min | 50% |

- Hybrid digital: questions on screen in Bluebook, free-response answers handwritten in a booklet.
- Calculators are permitted on both sections (check the approved list). A periodic table and an equations and constants sheet are provided.
- Reported on a 1–5 scale.

## Topic outline and exam weighting

| Unit (`id`) | Weight | Key ideas |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Atomic structure and properties (`atomic-structure`) | 7–9% | moles and molar mass, mass spectra, composition, electron configuration, photoelectron spectroscopy, periodic trends |
| 2. Compound structure and properties (`compound-structure`) | 7–10% | types of bonds, potential energy and bond length, ionic and metallic solids, Lewis structures, VSEPR, hybridization, resonance |
| 3. Intermolecular forces and properties of substances and mixtures (`imf-properties`) | 18–22% | intermolecular forces, solids, liquids and gases, ideal gas law, kinetic molecular theory, solutions, chromatography, spectroscopy, Beer–Lambert |
| 4. Chemical reactions (`chemical-reactions`) | 7–9% | net ionic equations, stoichiometry, titration, types of reactions |
| 5. Kinetics (`kinetics`) | 7–9% | rate laws, integrated rate laws, mechanisms, collision model, catalysis, energy profiles |
| 6. Thermochemistry (`thermochemistry`) | 7–9% | heat and temperature, calorimetry, enthalpy of reaction, bond enthalpies, Hess's law |
| 7. Equilibrium (`equilibrium`) | 7–9% | equilibrium constants, reaction quotient, ICE tables, Le Châtelier, solubility equilibria |
| 8. Acids and bases (`acids-bases`) | 11–15% | pH and pOH, strong and weak acids and bases, titration curves, buffers, pKa |
| 9. Thermodynamics and electrochemistry (`thermo-electrochem`) | 7–9% | entropy, Gibbs free energy and spontaneity, ΔG° and K, galvanic and electrolytic cells, Faraday's law |

## Skills the exam rewards

The CED's science practices: models and representations, question and method (experimental design), representing data and phenomena, model analysis, mathematical routines, and argumentation, which all appear in the free-response questions.

## Common traps

- Explaining with particle-level reasoning: "because it has stronger IMFs" without naming which forces and why.
- Significant figures and units in calculations; mixing up kJ and J, or °C and K.
- Equilibrium: writing solids or pure liquids into K expressions.
- Titrations: confusing the equivalence point with the half-equivalence point (where pH = pKa).
